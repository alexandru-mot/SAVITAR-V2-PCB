Torque, traction and the slip ceiling

Three limits decide whether a micro line follower reaches its target speed: what the tires can transmit, what the motor can produce, and how much of that the rotor eats accelerating itself.

Torque axis · per wheel
Logarithmic, mN·m. Shaded band above the slip line is unusable — commanding torque there just spins the tire.
Where the torque goes
Derived
Grip-limited acceleration is μ·g·f — independent of mass and wheel radius. A heavier robot needs more torque but gets proportionally more grip.
Wheel inertia modeled as a ring at 0.8·r. Rotor inertia reflected through the gearbox as I·N²·α and divided by efficiency. Stall torque is a theoretical extrapolation — sustained operation near it will overheat the windings. Rolling resistance, aerodynamic drag and weight transfer under acceleration are not modeled; on a lightweight chassis the first two are small, but weight transfer matters if your center of mass sits high or far from the axle.
